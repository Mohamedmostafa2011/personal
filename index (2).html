How the numbers work

Sleep happens in rounds

Night sleep repeats in cycles of light sleep, deep sleep and REM. Deep sleep dominates the first half of the night, while REM periods grow longer toward morning. Most people move through four to six rounds, and teenagers typically need more time in bed than adults.

Why duration matters

For ages 13–17 the recommended range is 8–10 hours a night. Regular timing matters too, but nights that consistently land short are associated with worse attention, mood and memory the following day.

Why cycles vary

Ninety minutes is an average used for planning. Real cycles run roughly 70 to 120 minutes, differ from person to person, and change during a single night — earlier rounds run longer, later ones shorter.

Why a cycle boundary is not a guarantee

Waking up right at the end of an estimated cycle is a planning idea, not a switch. Sleep inertia, your individual rhythm, stress, light and overall sleep debt all shape how refreshed a wake-up actually feels.
